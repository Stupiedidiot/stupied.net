This review is gonna be about both the book and the movie because the latter is rather faithful to it's source. I absolutely adore this story in concept. I think it highlights the difference between being moral with being courteous. The misogyny Starling has to go through is painful to say the least. She is constantly objectified and undermined, despite how much competence she showcase. The only person who treats people her as more than a pretty face is Hannibal. You know, the guy who literally eats people?? He's not interested in Starling's appearance. He's instead enraptured by her intelligence. Albeit in a "wonder if the rat can find their way out the maze" kind of way. He's trying to dig deep in her past to figure out why she is the way she is now and whether she has the courage to face what's ahead of her.

Despite my love for the concept, I can't help be annoyed by how much of a a drag this book is to read. It has the bad habit of repeating itself. It delivers information in lengthy ham fisted third-person info dumps only to reiterate the same information through character dialogue. Like hello??? I'm still fresh out of the previous chapter. Yes, I know Crawfords wife is dead. Yes, I know Buffalo Bill's legal name was a mistake. Can we please get the plot going? The constant change of perspective is also really frustrating and is detrimental to any sort of suspense the book trues to build up to. Honestly, just don't bother reading the book. The film does it a favor by cutting up the redundant stuff.

This story is... an acquired taste. I would not recommend it to other people.
