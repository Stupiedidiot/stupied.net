This is both about the book and the movie because the latter is rather faithful to it's source. I absolutely adore this story in concept. I think it highlights the difference between being moral with being courteous. The misogyny Starling has to go through is painful to say the least. She is constantly objectified and undermined, despite how much competence she showcase. The only person who treats people her as more than a pretty face is Hannibal. You know, the guy who literally eats people?? He's not interested in Starling's appearance. He's instead enraptured by her intelligence. Albeit in a "wonder if the rat can find their way out the maze" kind of way. He's trying to dig deep in her past to figure out why she is the way she is now and whether she has the courage to face what's ahead of her.

### The Movie
There isn't anything I can say about this film that hasn't been praised by millions of others. The casting and composition!!!! Particularly enjoy the focus on eyes. There are a lot panning shots with other detectives and cops judgingly looking up and down at her. It is such effective way of making the viewers experience the quiet discomforting misogyny Starling has to face. The movie is so so so much better than the book,, The film does it a favor by cutting up the redundancy and obfuscating character motives.

### The Book
Despite my love for the concept, I can't help be annoyed by how much of a drag the book is. It has the bad habit of repeating itself. It delivers information in lengthy ham fisted third-person info dumps only to reiterate the same information through character dialogue. Like hello??? I'm still fresh out of the previous chapter. Yes, I know Crawfords wife is dying. Yes, I know Buffalo Bill's legal name was a mistake. Can we please get the plot going? The constant change of perspective is also really frustrating and is detrimental to any sort of suspense the book tries to build up. It would have a chapter that's essentially "awoo wonder what Hannibal is plotting". As if his line of thinking wasn't already laid out in the chapter before. The only thing remotely of interest in this book is how intimately the investigation scenes are written. just don't bother reading the book. I'd give it an E ranking.
