> **prompt:** write a piece in which someone is mistaken about you.

"Well, that's easy for *you* to say". It was clear that my comment about how easy it was to make friends hit a sensitive nerve in Kooky. She crosses her arms and pouts like a little kid. It was an embarrassing sight to see. Has she not internalized the fact that we were junior high school students now? You'd think by now she's understood she's far too old to be accessorizing in chunky beaded bracelets and handmade loom band charms. "Everyone in class loves you. You get invited to everything." There was a bit of truth to her statement. Whenever the teacher instructs everyone to form groups of three, there would always be a group missing a member. Usually, it be Kooky paired with the unfortunate kid who didn't rush to their friend group fast enough. But sometimes exceptions were made, and a single group would be granted the privilege of an extra member. The other top achievers in my class have made it a point to recruit me for every project. One was a girl older than me who would show off her pin collection of whatever trending anime she hasn't watched yet. The other was a guy who would constantly complain about being in a situationship with someone who wasn't ready to come out. I'm not exactly sure what compelled me on that day, but I turned their offer down and insisted to pair with Kooky. It took a while to convince them that I wasn't being coerced, nor had I forgotten what happened back in elementary. The teacher praised me for my selflessness while Kooky got scolded when she claimed she'd rather do the project alone. So that brings us to now; Inside a mostly empty library at 6PM. The only noise accompanying us was the repeated clicking of Kooky's multi-colored pen. "They don't love me. They love what I can offer.” Her eyes roll to the back of her head before it goes back to attending to the unicorns she had doodle out in her notebook. The project was fairly simple. Survey other students about their English grades from last year and analyze the data. Getting things like the mean, median and variance then interpreting those results. One of the many concepts my parents forced me to study in advance during the summer, We would probably finish a lot sooner if I did all the work myself. "You're pretty lucky since no one bothers you for anything". 

"Okay, I get it. I'm useless". Although it wasn't my intention, the tone of my remarked motivated her to finally snatch the index card I laid out for her. Written on it was the formula to get the median. It's quite a miracle she manage to graduate from elementary. Not because she lack of intelligence but because of her rather problematic behaviour. Everyone in my class knows what happened on that day. The part they always leave out is the reason why it started in the first place. It was during one of my verbal back and forth with Kooky. I comment on the usual things like how messy her hair was and how tacky all her clothing were. Kooky had grown so used to them that she had a comeback prepared for each insult. It annoyed me to no end. Blind by a burning desire to see her knocked down a few pegs, I yelled "at least I have a *real* dad"; Which is a horrible thing to say to an adopted child. Kooky lunged at me, tugged on my hair, and dragged my face against the wired mesh wall. A guard nearby interfered before the situation exacerbated. Feeling a slight guilt of my past recklessness I say "you're dad loves you". Perhaps the memory of the incident was still deeply integrated in her mind since her brows furrowed and she stumbles on her next words. "I mean yeah. He's my parent. He kinda has to". Finding her reasoning amusing I say "That's strange. Guess mine didn't catch the memo". Kooky didn't reply back because she was preoccupied with booting her calculator up. It was the same model as mine. Mentally, I debated whether or not to tell her about the statistic mode and concluded not to out of pettiness. "You know, I'm still upset about that day". Kooky doesn't even bother looking up at me while she responds with "Well, whatever. You had it coming." Seeing how focus she was with manually encoding the formula unto the calculator, I decided not to bother correcting her. Truthfully, I was never upset about the what happened but more so of what happened afterwards. Her dad worked in the campus and arrived quite quickly when the guidance office called him over. I felt a deep sense of pride seeing with how sternly she got told off by him. But that satisfaction very quickly waned when his gaze soften at the sight of her choking on her tears. He knelt down, gently picked her up, and they took off with a promise to stop by for some ice cream. During the car ride back home I kept wondering why my parents were so fixated about how the scar on my face would look on my portfolio. 

Feeling rather worn off the day, I laid my head down on the table and let my hand mindlessly tip toe towards Kooky. Her left hand was rested quite closely to my side of desk. She didn't seem to complain about me inserting my finger between the nylon of bracelet and her bare skin; Tugging it upwards ever so lightly and letting go. "I envy you, Kooky. I really do."
